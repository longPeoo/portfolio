I came all the way to Italy to eat spaghetti, pizza and pasta, but honestly, the only thing I truly loved was the Italian ice cream; the rest was fine, just not quite wow enough for me. Italian gelato, though, was absolutely superb. I had to eat three a day, each from a different shop, just to get the full experience lol.

Well, Italy is the homeland of the ancient Roman Empire, one of the cradles of the Renaissance, and the land of great names like Leonardo da Vinci and Michelangelo. So calling Italy a living museum of history and art is no exaggeration.

On this journey I travelled from northern to central Italy, starting in Venice, then on to Milan, a teeny tiny stop in Pisa, and finally Rome. In Pisa I even went in to see the tower while dragging my suitcase along behind me the whole time, it was hilarious, but I only wanted a quick look to see just how far it leans.

Venice, of course, is truly special, just like the waterways of Vietnam's Mekong Delta, only with different architecture. A place where public transport can mean boats gliding across the water.

After that I stopped by the fashion capital of the world, because I love beautiful things. Milan has the Duomo di Milano, the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II shopping arcade, and even the spot where you put your heel on the bull and spin for good luck, but I didn't spin, and I wish I had hehe. Next time I'll definitely come back and spin ten times!

Then came Pisa, home of the famous leaning tower that you've probably seen countless times in travel photos. I think it's one of the few buildings in the world that gets more famous the more it leans. If the tower had stood straight from the start, there might not be so many people coming to take photos of themselves pushing it like there are now, hehe.

And my final stop in Italy was Rome, the capital of the boot-shaped country. Here I collected my first of the New Seven Wonders of the World, the Colosseum, so that's 1 out of 7. It'll be amazing when I get to 7 out of 7.
