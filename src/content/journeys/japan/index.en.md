On 29 August 2024 I set foot in Japan for the very first time, the land of Doraemon, whom probably all of us grew up with. My starting point was Osaka, Japan's famous food city, and the first thing on the list was, of course, checking in at Osaka Castle. The white and green castle sits on top of massive stone walls, surrounded by a moat, and standing below looking up I kept imagining the samurai standing guard in the old days.

Nara is truly a paradise for deer: they wander everywhere, lie sunbathing in front of temple gates and follow tourists around asking for crackers. The ones that get crackers even bow back, more polite than people, honestly. That day it kept switching between sunshine and rain, and I held my umbrella while playing with the deer, as happy as a little kid hehe.

Kyoto is just as timeless as people say. I arrived at Kiyomizu-dera right at sunset, with the whole sky turning pink behind the bright red gate, so beautiful that I stood still and couldn't say a word. Then I wandered down the old streets, very crowded and yet still peaceful, before stopping by Kinkaku-ji, the Golden Pavilion, its gold leaf sparkling and reflected in the pond.

My favourite destination of all, though, was Amanohashidate, one of the Three Views of Japan. It's a long sandbar covered in pine forest stretching across a bay, and seen from the hill above it looks like a bridge reaching up to the sky. They say you have to bend over and look at it upside down between your legs to see the bridge truly float up into the sky, so give it a try if you go, just keep your balance or you'll roll down the hill lol.

Six days in Japan flew by. I came home with a backpack full of photos and a head full of things I want to learn from the people there, from cleanliness and punctuality to respect for others. I promised myself I'd come back, and less than a year later I really did, but this time on a business trip to Tokyo.
