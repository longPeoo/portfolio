My flight from Rome landed in Budapest at 10 p.m. on 24 August 2023, and I dragged my suitcase to the homestay with my eyes drooping shut. But the next morning, the moment I opened the door I was wide awake: Budapest is beautiful in a very different way, both timeless and grand, just like the "magnificent Budapest" I wrote about in my Bratislava post.

25 August was my only full day in Budapest, so I walked non-stop. Matthias Church, Fisherman's Bastion, Heroes' Square, a climb up to the Citadella, then a walk across the Chain Bridge; my legs ached but I couldn't stop marvelling. Did you know the city is split in two by the Danube? Buda is the hilly side with the castle, Pest is flatter and livelier, and together they make Budapest.

Looking down from Fisherman's Bastion, the whole city stretched out along both banks of the deep blue Danube, and I stood there gazing for ages before I could tear myself away. As for the Parliament Building, it needs no introduction: it's one of the largest parliament buildings in the world. I walked all the way around it photographing every angle, and on the morning of 26 August, before heading to the station, I made a point of going back for one last look.

There's one spot I want to talk about on its own: the iron shoes lined up along the bank of the Danube. It's a memorial to the Jews who were murdered on the riverbank during the Second World War; they were made to take off their shoes before being shot. Standing there under a beautiful sunny sky, my heart felt heavy, and I suddenly felt grateful for the peaceful life I have.

At 11:29 a.m. that day I boarded the train to Bratislava. I spent less than two days in Budapest and had already fallen for it. I'll definitely come back to soak in the thermal baths and properly see the Danube at night.
