24 August 2023 was my last day in Rome. I checked out at noon and had a flight to Budapest at 8:15 p.m., so I made the most of the afternoon to visit one more country: Vatican City. By 2:27 p.m. I was already standing in the queue in the middle of St. Peter's Square, the August sun beating straight down on my head, and the line still stretched on and on.

Vatican City is the smallest country in the world, less than half a square kilometre in area, tucked right in the middle of Rome. The funniest part is that there's no border post at all. I just walked from Italy into Vatican City with no visa and nobody stamping my passport; all it takes is stepping over a white line on the paving stones and you're in another country. The curved stone colonnade wraps around the square like a pair of open arms, and on top of it hundreds of statues of saints look down.

The moment I stepped into St. Peter's Basilica, I froze. The gilded ceiling soars high above, light slants in through the windows, and everywhere you look there are statues and paintings. I stopped longest in front of Michelangelo's Pietà; he carved it when he was only about 24, and now it sits behind a protective pane of glass. Standing there at 23, I wondered what I'd manage to do for the world by next year lol.

At the gate I met the Swiss Guards on duty in their eye-catching blue, yellow and red striped uniforms. I had left Switzerland only three days earlier, and here I was in Vatican City meeting Swiss people again. It must be fate.

After leaving Vatican City, I still had time to stop by the Pantheon and Castel Sant'Angelo before dragging my suitcase to the airport. Vatican City is so tiny that whenever I count the countries on my 15-day trip I keep forgetting this little buddy, but small as it is, Vatican City is still a proper country in my flag collection.
